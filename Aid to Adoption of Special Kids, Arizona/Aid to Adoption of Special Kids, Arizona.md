# Aid to Adoption of Special Kids, Arizona

Not an FY25 funded partner of Valley of the Sun United Way.

## About

Aid to Adoption of Special Kids, commonly known as AASK, is a private nonprofit foster care and adoption organization serving children and families in Arizona's child welfare system. The organization was founded in 1988 to help ensure that children in foster care have committed, caring adults in their lives and are connected to stable family relationships.

AASK was founded by philanthropist and child advocate Katherine "Kax" Herberger, whose core belief was that every child needs a home and a family regardless of background, trauma history, or special needs. Since its founding, AASK has focused on children in foster care who need enduring relationships, permanency, advocacy, and support from adults who understand the emotional effects of abuse, neglect, separation, and instability.

The organization provides foster care, adoption, kinship foster care, mentoring, and family support services. Its foster care work helps recruit, train, license, and support families who can care for children placed away from their biological parents, often with the goal of reunification. Its adoption services help children in Arizona's foster care system find permanent homes with families prepared to meet their needs. AASK also provides ongoing guidance and post-placement advocacy so families are not left to navigate the child welfare system alone.

AASK's kinship foster care program supports relatives such as grandparents, aunts, uncles, and siblings who unexpectedly become caregivers for children placed in foster care. The program offers system navigation and practical support even before a family is fully licensed, helping caregivers understand the child welfare process and stabilize the placement.

Through mentoring, AASK pairs committed adults with youth in foster care, including children and teens who need reliable connections as they move through adolescence and prepare for adulthood. The organization also works with community partners and media partnerships, including Wednesday's Child, to raise awareness of children waiting for adoptive families.

AASK serves children with varied needs and backgrounds, including Latino children, American Indian children, children with developmental disabilities, youth with complex histories, and young people who may otherwise risk aging out of foster care without a lasting adult connection. The organization is licensed by the Arizona Department of Child Safety and is based in Phoenix at 2320 N. 20th Street.

## Mission & Vision

AASK's mission is to serve children in Arizona's foster care system by ensuring that every child has someone who cares deeply about them. Its work is grounded in the belief that stable, lasting relationships help children heal from trauma, restore trust, and improve their chances for lifelong success.

The organization's vision is that every foster child should have someone who cares. AASK frames permanency and family connection as essential to a child's future, not optional extras. Its programs are designed to help children build trusting relationships that can nurture them into responsible, productive adults.

AASK also emphasizes that relationships are hard work and that foster, adoptive, kinship, and mentoring families need strong support to succeed. Its service model is built around making the difficult work of fostering and adopting easier for families through training, advocacy, licensing help, system navigation, and ongoing specialist support.
