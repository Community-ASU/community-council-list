# Foundation for Blind Children

Is an FY25 funded partner of Valley of the Sun United Way.

## About

Foundation for Blind Children, commonly known as FBC, is an Arizona nonprofit serving people who are blind or visually impaired. Founded in 1952 by families seeking a brighter future for children with vision loss, FBC has grown into Arizona's only agency of its kind and serves more than 2,000 people of all ages, from newborns to seniors over 100.

FBC provides education, tools, services, resources, guidance, and encouragement so individuals with vision loss can build independence and participate more fully in school, work, home, and community life. The organization believes vision loss is not a limitation, but an opportunity to approach life differently with the right support, technology, training, and confidence.

The organization's services span early childhood, school-age education, youth development, accessible learning resources, adult services, low vision services, and research. Its Early Intervention program supports infants and toddlers with visual impairments through home visits, developmental guidance, family-centered goals, assessments, assistive tools, and strategies that help children engage and grow.

FBC also operates preschool and elementary school-based programs for children with vision loss. These programs build pre-academic, pre-Braille, reading, mobility, communication, and daily living skills through adapted curriculum, classroom supports, occupational therapy, speech and language therapy, physical therapy, music, pet therapy, and instruction aligned with the Expanded Core Curriculum and Arizona standards.

For youth and young adults, FBC offers independence, career, and college readiness programs. These include summer residential experiences, weekend workshops, job readiness training, daily living skills, orientation and mobility, assistive technology, paid internships, mentoring, self-advocacy, leadership development, and postsecondary planning.

FBC also provides statewide accessible learning resources through the Arizona Instructional Resource Center, including braille, large print, and digital educational materials. Its Low Vision Clinic helps people of all ages access low vision evaluations, magnification tools, daily living aids, and strategies to make the most of remaining vision. The organization's research work advances knowledge and services for families, educators, and individuals affected by visual impairment.

## Mission & Vision

Foundation for Blind Children's mission is to provide education, tools, and services that enable all persons with vision loss to achieve greater independence.

Its vision is to have a world where vision loss is a diagnosis, not a disability.

The organization is guided by core values that include exceptional service, advocacy, financial responsibility, a great work environment, and community partnerships. Across its programs, FBC emphasizes independence, access, dignity, education, adaptive tools, family support, innovation, and opportunity for individuals with vision loss throughout every stage of life.
